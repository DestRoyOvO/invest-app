# Signal evaluation

Generated by `npm run evaluate` (scripts/evaluate-signal.ts) for model v4, from the settings pinned in scripts/evaluation.config.json. Re-running on the same cached bars (`npm run evaluate -- --offline`) reproduces this file and lib/signal-evaluation.json exactly.

## What is measured

- Universe: 78 large US stocks from the sector heatmap (lib/universe.ts). A stock enters once it has 120 sessions of history.
- Signal: the technical score, recomputed for every session from data up to that session (the live code path). It combines momentum, trend, RSI and volume; the volatility regime is still computed but has no weight. Readings use the live cut-offs (very strong >= 72, strong >= 58, weak <= 42, very weak <= 28).
- Window: signals from 2016-01-04 to 2026-11-06 (pinned 2016-01-01 to 2026-11-06). Bars after the window end are not used, not even as exit prices, so the last 20 sessions only count at the shorter horizons.
- Returns: bought at the next session's open, sold at the close 5 / 10 / 20 sessions after the signal, dividend and split adjusted, before costs. A date counts when at least 20 stocks have a forward return.

## Readings and what followed

Each reading's mean excess return over all observations splits exactly into **timing** (the same-day universe was up or down when the reading occurred) and **selection** (the stock against the equal-weight universe on the same day). Selection is the part a stock picker could use. Readings cluster in sell-offs and rallies, so the intervals come from a moving-block bootstrap over dates (20-session blocks, 2000 replicates, seed 20261107); an episode is a run of dates with gaps of at most 10 sessions.

## Holdout decade

Signals 2014-01-02 to 2024-04-29, the decade before the window the model was first evaluated on (2024-04-30 to 2026-11-06). The rule was fixed before the run: primary horizon 10 sessions, all-dates rank IC with a Newey-West t, one-sided in the designed direction (a higher score should precede a higher return), the composite tested at 5% and the five technical factors with Holm's adjustment at 5%. A factor that fails gets no weight in a directional reading. Dropping the volatility regime from the score used this table, so for the current model the decade is no longer a clean holdout; removing a factor with an IC near zero cannot create an edge, and the per-factor tests are unchanged.

## Method notes

- Rank IC: on each date, the Spearman correlation between the score and the forward return across stocks; the mean over all dates is tested with a Newey-West t (Bartlett kernel, lag h, because h-session returns overlap). Every h-th date gives a non-overlapping grid, one per starting phase; the range of their classical t shows how much a single grid could mislead, and the verdict is flagged fragile when the grids disagree.
- A relationship counts as reliable when |t| >= 2. The label table overlaps heavily, so read it through its intervals, dates and episodes.
- The per-stock backtest on each quote page adds a circular-shift permutation p-value per hold period (lib/signal.ts), because across 78 stocks some edges look large by chance.

## Caveats

- Survivorship and selection: the universe is today's large caps; the hindsight gap above measures how much that flatters returns.
- One market: a strong US bull market with a few sharp sell-offs. Short-term reversal effects in large caps come and go with volatility.
- Before costs, no slippage. The technical score only: fundamentals have no point-in-time history here.

## Data

Yahoo daily charts via yahoo-finance2, from 2013-01-01 (the earlier bars warm up the score) to 2026-11-06. 78 stocks plus SPY and RSP; last bar 2026-11-06. Shorter histories: CEG (1206). The bar count and last date of every symbol are in lib/signal-evaluation.json (data.fingerprint). Yahoo recomputes adjusted closes in single precision on every request, so a fresh download can move the fourth or fifth decimal of these statistics; the cached bars reproduce them exactly.
